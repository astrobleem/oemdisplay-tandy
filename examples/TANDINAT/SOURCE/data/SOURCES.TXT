PROVENANCE AND CURATION NOTES
=============================
Prepared 2026-10-05 for the original TANDINATOR starter roster.

The questions, representation and answer matrix were composed for this
project using general knowledge and the targeted primary-reference checks
below. No Akinator database, question bank, response history, API, extracted
service content or proprietary training set was copied or queried. No
character art, logos, movie dialogue or biography passages are included.
Character names and trademarks belong to their respective owners; this
project does not imply endorsement by any rightsholder.

The complete 16,384-cell matrix has not been independently source-verified
cell by cell. Most negative classifications mean the broad trait is not
part of the character's familiar/main portrayal. They are not a claim that
no obscure adaptation ever depicts it. General-knowledge entries are
explicitly labeled general in STARTER.JSON. Each source entry states the
scope of its check; a reference link is not represented as verifying every
answer for all characters from that franchise.

PORTRAYAL CONVENTIONS
--------------------
* A fictional entry uses its familiar version, documented in its notes.
  Disney Mulan, Ariel, Belle and Aladdin refer to familiar film portrayals;
  Thor is Marvel's character. Shrek and Donkey are DreamWorks characters,
  not Disney properties. Origin questions concern the named portrayal.
* The origin medium is different from later adaptations. Popeye, Snoopy,
  Charlie Brown and Garfield originated in comic strips; Winnie the Pooh,
  Tigger and Shrek originated in books; Harley Quinn in animation.
* Fictional debut bands refer to real publication/release chronology.
  Yoda, Garfield, Frodo and Aragorn were explicitly checked for band errors
  during curation. Historical-person questions refer to actual human lives
  and public activity, not the date of a movie adaptation.
* Folkloric Santa, Merlin, Robin Hood and King Arthur are fictional/legendary
  roster entries. The game does not adjudicate religious belief or assert
  archaeological proof of a specific historical version of a legend.
* Human classification concerns species, not a humanoid silhouette. Link
  and Zelda are left unknown; Frodo is a hobbit; Superman is an alien.
  Toy Story figures are toys. The Hulk and several magical figures have
  unknown values where form or interpretation changes the answer.
* Appearance concerns an iconic portrayal rather than every costume.
  Real performers' variable outfit colors are generally unknown. Skin
  color questions refer to plainly fantastical colors, never ethnicity.
  No medical, financial or private-life speculation is encoded.
* Red_hair explicitly includes orange, including orange animal fur.
  Hat excludes helmets, which have a separate question. Biological ears
  are different from points on a superhero's cowl. Flight means flying
  without an ordinary aircraft; a real-world pilot is not supernatural.
* Powerful humans are not automatically superhuman. Mr. T, Hulk Hogan and
  Muhammad Ali do not receive extraordinary-strength ability merely for
  being athletic or imposing. Competitive wrestling is included in sport.
* UNKNOWN and probable values deliberately retain ambiguity. The matrix
  is editable, and a user can answer unknown without making a false claim.

QUALITY AND LIMITS
------------------
Every question has more than one answer value across the roster. Every
character profile is distinct. The closest profiles differ at three
positions, so the game must handle rejected guesses and mistaken answers.
The roster includes different media, genders, eras, settings and geographic
associations, but is weighted toward recognizable English-language popular
culture and Japanese games. It is a starter, not a representative census.
The binary tests prove structure and reproducibility, not universal factual
accuracy or equal recognition by every user.
